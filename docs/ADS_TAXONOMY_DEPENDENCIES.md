# Sponsored Advertising — Taxonomy Dependencies

**State:** PROPOSED FOR PRODUCT OWNER REVIEW
**Scope:** Design only; no runtime, database, ranking, billing, or taxonomy mutation.

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how sponsored advertising may consume EsnaftaVar's Product Taxonomy, Merchant/Sector Taxonomy, facets, and search vocabulary without making advertising identity depend on mutable category labels or paths.

The advertising engine is a consumer of taxonomy context, not an owner of taxonomy truth.

## 2. Dependency Principles

1. Campaign, ad group, creative, and sponsored-object identities use their own immutable opaque IDs.
2. A `SHOP_LISTING` remains the proposed V1 sponsored object. Taxonomy nodes describe context; they do not replace the listing.
3. Targeting references must eventually use immutable taxonomy node IDs plus the taxonomy revision observed when the target was saved.
4. Turkish display names, slugs, aliases, and parent paths are mutable presentation/search data and must not be used as durable advertising identity.
5. Product Taxonomy and Merchant Taxonomy are independent. A merchant sector never proves that a particular product listing is eligible.
6. Facets narrow contextual relevance but cannot create unsupported product, health, certification, compatibility, or price claims.
7. Every ad request must re-check the current listing, merchant, shop, policy, budget, and locality state. A stored taxonomy match is not an eligibility grant.

## 3. Product Taxonomy Dependency

Product Taxonomy may support:

- contextual retrieval by canonical category;
- category-page placement;
- query-to-category interpretation;
- policy classification and review routing;
- reporting grouped by stable category identity;
- future negative category targeting.

It must not:

- sponsor a category node as if it were an available product;
- infer a listing's existence, price, stock, or seller authority;
- allow a parent category match to bypass exact listing checks;
- turn an attribute or brand into a category merely for targeting convenience.

### 3.1 Target lifecycle

| Taxonomy change | Proposed advertising response |
|---|---|
| Display-name or slug rename | Preserve the target by stable ID; refresh presentation; retain old term only as alias/search vocabulary. |
| Move with unchanged semantic identity | Preserve stable ID; re-evaluate placement scope and policy context before serving. |
| Merge | Map predecessor IDs to the approved successor; pause ambiguous or policy-sensitive campaigns until reviewed. |
| Split | Do not arbitrarily choose one child. Pause affected target and require deterministic reassignment or merchant review. |
| Retire | Stop new serving to the node; retain historical reporting against the retired ID. |
| Proposal revision before owner finalization | Treat the target as provisional; do not freeze runtime contracts against it. |

The 24 L1 Product Taxonomy names are owner-final. Only owner-final L2/L3/L4 domains may be treated as canonical targets; proposal-only branches remain provisional inputs.

## 4. Merchant/Sector Taxonomy Dependency

Merchant/Sector Taxonomy may support:

- onboarding and policy review routing;
- broad contextual eligibility hints;
- sector-level reporting;
- detecting obvious cross-sector risk;
- future merchant discovery or awareness campaigns.

It must not:

- authorize an ad solely because the shop selected a sector;
- substitute for product-level evidence or legal verification;
- force a product into the merchant's primary sector;
- create a ranking advantage by sector alone;
- silently inherit a proposed sector as a final policy class.

The current merchant architecture has 67 proposed assignable leaves. Its verification and legal-review signals are inputs to review, not automatic approvals or prohibitions. Until owner finalization and stable IDs exist, merchant-sector targets are `PROVISIONAL_PROPOSAL`.

## 5. Facet Dependency

Facet values may improve contextual matching for attributes such as size, compatibility, capacity, material, connector, or product condition. Proposed rules:

- facet identity must be scoped to its canonical node and schema revision;
- structured values outrank free-text claims;
- unknown, stale, or conflicting facet values must not be positively inferred;
- a facet can narrow an eligible candidate set but cannot revive an ineligible listing;
- sensitive or regulated claims require the relevant evidence and policy state outside the facet system;
- targeting a facet does not guarantee delivery volume.

Compatibility is especially fail-closed: a `compatible`, `incompatible`, `conditional`, or `unknown` relationship remains a product/catalog fact, not an advertising claim generated by targeting.

## 6. Search Vocabulary, Synonyms, and Aliases

The engine must distinguish:

- **Search synonym:** a customer-language term used for intent understanding.
- **Legacy alias/redirect:** an old taxonomy identity or slug retained for continuity.
- **Ad keyword/intent target:** a campaign selection rule.

These may share text but are not interchangeable records. A keyword match cannot override an explicit negative target, category exclusion, policy block, or exact product mismatch. Search-synonym expansion should be logged with its source revision so explanation and dispute review can reconstruct why a candidate was retrieved.

## 7. Versioning Contract

Every saved taxonomy-dependent target should conceptually record:

- advertising target ID;
- referenced taxonomy system (`PRODUCT`, `MERCHANT`, or `FACET`);
- immutable referenced node/value ID;
- taxonomy revision at creation/update;
- inclusion or exclusion mode;
- resolution state (`ACTIVE`, `STALE`, `AMBIGUOUS`, `RETIRED`, `POLICY_REVIEW`);
- last resolution timestamp and reason.

Serving uses the latest approved taxonomy/policy snapshot while preserving the campaign's historical revision for audit. Reporting must not rewrite historical events when names or paths change.

## 8. Failure and Fallback

If taxonomy resolution fails, is stale, or becomes ambiguous:

- do not broaden the target silently;
- do not charge for an unverified sponsored impression;
- suppress the affected ad candidate;
- preserve the unmodified organic result set;
- surface a merchant/admin review reason;
- retain an auditable resolution event without exposing internal policy signals to customers.

## 9. Cross-System Ownership

| Concern | Authoritative owner | Advertising use |
|---|---|---|
| Product hierarchy | Product Taxonomy | Context, retrieval, policy routing, reporting |
| Merchant type | Merchant Taxonomy | Weak eligibility/review context |
| Product/listing facts | Catalog and shop listing | Sponsored-object truth and freshness |
| Attribute semantics | Facet architecture | Contextual refinement |
| Search intent | Search architecture | Query interpretation and retrieval evidence |
| Ad eligibility | Advertising policy layer | Final serve/no-serve decision |
| Display order | Sponsored ranking layer | Limited interleave after independent organic ranking |

## 10. Open Owner Decisions

1. Which taxonomy depths may merchants target in V1: L1/L2 only or owner-final leaves as well?
2. Should category targeting be inclusion-only in V1, or also support explicit negative targets?
3. How long may a stale target remain paused before automatic campaign cancellation or refund/credit review?
4. Which owner-final taxonomy revision mechanism becomes the advertising compatibility contract?
5. May Merchant Taxonomy ever be merchant-selectable targeting, or remain review/reporting context only?

## 11. Recommendation

For V1, allow only owner-final Product Taxonomy stable targets after stable IDs exist, use Merchant Taxonomy only as an eligibility/review signal, and keep facets as contextual refinements. Any split, unresolved proposal, or policy-sensitive taxonomy change should fail closed for the sponsored candidate while organic discovery continues unchanged.
