# EsnaftaVar Merchant Sector Local-Retail Realism Audit

**State:** PROPOSAL AUDIT — NO OWNER FINALIZATION

## 1. Audit lens

The V1 proposal is judged as a Turkish neighbourhood-merchant acquisition and
customer-discovery system, not as a giant marketplace department tree. Each family
is scored on:

- merchant recognition of the name;
- customer recognition of the name;
- presence of common standalone/micro businesses;
- fit for mixed inventory and service realities;
- onboarding burden;
- risk of Product Taxonomy leakage;
- regulatory clarity.

Evidence includes current TÜİK/Ministry NACE signals, TESK profession language, the
prior 46 merchant-archetype Product Taxonomy test, this wave's 180 stress cases and
100 edge cases. Ratings are research judgements, not statistical prevalence claims.

## 4. What feels locally authentic

The highest-confidence leaf vocabulary is the language heard on storefronts and in
ordinary search:

- bakkal/market, kasap, şarküteri, manav, fırın, pastane, kuruyemişçi, aktar;
- giyimci, ayakkabıcı, çantacı;
- telefoncu, elektronikçi, bilgisayarcı, beyaz eşyacı;
- mobilyacı, züccaciye, halıcı, perdeci;
- nalbur, elektrik malzemecisi, tesisat malzemecisi, oto parçacı, lastikçi;
- kozmetikçi, berber, kadın kuaförü, güzellik salonu;
- kitapçı, kırtasiye, oyuncakçı, pet shop, optikçi, kuyumcu, saatçi,
  medikalci and çiçekçi;
- terzi, ayakkabı tamircisi, anahtarcı, kuru temizlemeci and device services.

These are more natural than raw NACE descriptions, even when a formal code is
linked behind the scenes.

## 5. Micro-merchant fit

- A one-person shop can choose the same leaf as a larger local store; size is not a
  sector.
- A market can stock five Product L1s without five merchant sectors.
- A service merchant can operate in-store or in a service area; location mode is
  metadata.
- Home/workshop-only activity needs future location/eligibility rules but not a
  duplicate sector.
- Seasonal merchants retain their durable identity instead of switching every
  campaign.

## 6. Giant-marketplace assumptions avoided

- No brand-as-sector or one-node-per-product family.
- No gender, price tier, style, target audience or inventory volume as hierarchy.
- Only three L3 leaves, all required by an owner-confirmed subtree.
- Primary plus up to three secondaries replaces combinatorial hybrid nodes.
- Product search remains Product Taxonomy based.
- Family nodes are navigation aids, not forced merchant assignments.

## 7. Onboarding confusion points

1. `Market, Bakkal & Süpermarket` combines scale labels; the definition must say the
   scale does not change eligibility.
2. Retail plus repair (`telefoncu`, `bilgisayarcı`, `bisikletçi`) needs a plain
   primary/secondary example.
3. `Hediyelik Eşya Mağazası` must explain that gift stock can span product domains.
4. `Optik, Saat, Takı & Medikal` should not imply one shared licence.
5. `Tamir, Bakım & Yerel Hizmetler` contains varied services; search should land on
   a leaf instead of forcing family browsing.
6. `Other` must collect an unresolved request rather than publish free text.

## 8. Missing-local-business signal

The 180-case test intentionally found eight unmatched or out-of-current-scope local
business types: pharmacy, veterinary clinic, cafe/restaurant, automotive repair,
photography studio, real-estate office, custom carpenter and equipment rental.

They do **not** automatically become nodes. Product Owner must decide whether Wave
16 targets product-adjacent merchants only or a broader local-services directory.
That single scope decision has higher value than adding these piecemeal.

## 9. Conclusion

The proposal is credible for product-adjacent neighbourhood retail and a controlled
set of services. It is not yet a comprehensive directory of every local profession.
Most leaf names are acquisition-ready; three family areas and six granularity nodes
need substantive owner review before finalization.
