# EsnaftaVar Esenler Pilot — Contrarian Launch Review

**State:** `INDEPENDENT CHALLENGE — RECOMMENDATIONS NOT FINAL`

| Prior assumption/challenge | Contrary finding | Recommendation | Risk if wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full Merchant App must finish first | Capabilities matter; a controlled audited path can serve a tiny cohort. | Do not wait for full app if minimum authority/catalog/QR path passes. | Manual path becomes permanent or unsafe. |
| 100 merchants before launch | Count does not prove density, truth or supportability. | Launch smaller in one useful cell; expand on evidence. | Sparse selection limits customer value. |
| Fewer merchants means weak pilot | Concentration can improve choice and support learning. | Prefer `12–25` ready shops over scattered registrations. | Selection bias and narrow domains. |
| Three months free is optimal | It may defer willingness-to-pay learning and create an entitlement expectation. | Compare milestone/research/free-basic/paid hypotheses. | Early price friction reduces recruitment. |
| Meta is enough for acquisition | Paid reach cannot repair thin supply or prove organic usefulness. | Merchant/community/referral first; bounded Meta later. | Organic channels may scale slowly. |
| One-person operation is cheap | Hidden owner time, interruptions and no backup can make it unsafe. | One trained operator only with strict caps and named escalation/backup. | Fixed staffing cost too early. |
| Ads/Rewards improve launch | They add policy, billing, fraud, liability and distort core behavior. | Defer all; test core discovery naturally. | Missed early engagement/revenue signal. |
| Android+iOS both required | iOS is a current readiness gap; dual launch widens QA/support. | Android-first if owner accepts exclusion and field device evidence. | Cohort bias/excluded customers. |
| Complete catalog is required | A truthful deep subset can validate discovery. | Promise selected domains/cells, not completeness. | Users seek missing categories. |
| QR must launch day one | Discovery and merchant truth can be tested first; QR adds counter workflow. | Limited QR cohort or staged start. | Delays review/verified-purchase learning. |
| Support/call center needed immediately | Enterprise contact-center capacity is excessive for a tiny controlled cohort. | Declared-hours lean support with incident route. | Critical issues arrive out of hours. |
| Broad public launch gives best evidence | It can overwhelm supply/support and damage trust before learning. | Invite/closed then bounded public. | Invite cohort is unrepresentative. |
| Every Product L1 must appear | Empty labels harm trust and proposed L2s are not all final. | Launch only useful ordinary domains. | Narrow value proposition. |
| Verified purchases equal commercial success | QR coverage is partial and no payment/revenue is proven. | Treat as one authoritative behavior KPI with caveats. | Underestimates value outside QR. |
| Automate support/catalog early | Automation without labeled cases can amplify wrong decisions. | Manual governed baseline, automate repeated safe work. | Operator capacity caps growth. |

## Strongest conclusion

The pilot's probability improves when it is treated as a controlled operating
experiment, not a compressed district-wide product launch. The smallest coherent
configuration excludes Ads, Reward/Gamification, composite reputation, regulated
domains, broad iOS promise and full-catalog completeness.
