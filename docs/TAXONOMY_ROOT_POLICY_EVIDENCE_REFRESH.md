## Method and evidence limits

- Only Turkish government, regulator and official gazette sources were used for the current-policy refresh.
- A rule is treated as taxonomy-relevant only when it changes product eligibility, evidence fields, seller eligibility, fulfilment or safety handling.
- Legal status is not inferred from a product name, customer-facing label or marketplace placement.
- Where a source regulates activity rather than taxonomy, that limitation is stated explicitly.
- A source confirming that a product family is regulated does not decide its EsnaftaVar L1 owner.
- Unknown or mixed-status items remain excluded or review-gated until an exact SKU-level rule exists.

## Root-by-root refresh

### ROOT-01 — Medical intended-use rule

- **Current official support:** TR-01 shows that medical-device classification is tied to governing device scope and intended use, with registration/sales obligations. TR-02 separately shows that supplements are approved food products, not medicines, and may not carry treatment/prevention claims.
- **Taxonomy consequence:** Product form, intended medical function, regulated status and authoritative registration/approval evidence must be captured separately. Marketing language cannot promote an ordinary product into medical ownership.
- **Fail-closed gate:** A claimed medical device, medical accessory, supplement or medical nutrition item without verifiable status remains in review or excluded; it must not be routed by title keywords alone.
- **Evidence required later:** Exact product class, intended use, registration/approval identifier, authorized seller status where applicable, claims and age restrictions.
- **Unresolved:** The exact item-level eligibility matrix and the L1 owner for mixed wellness/medical products.
- **Recommendation impact:** Supports ROOT-01 Option A; does not make it owner-final.

### ROOT-02 — Baby/life-stage ownership rule

- **Current official support:** TR-03 confirms infant/follow-on formula has specific official food guidance. TR-02 confirms an under-two restriction in the supplement-approval context.
- **Taxonomy consequence:** Baby nutrition can require a genuine life-stage schema; an age label alone does not create the same regulatory/product identity for apparel, bags, footwear or toys.
- **Fail-closed gate:** Formula, supplement and health/safety claims require exact product-family evidence. Do not generalize a baby-specific rule to all smaller-sized products.
- **Evidence required later:** Intended age, formula/food/supplement status, mandatory labelling and seller/product eligibility.
- **Unresolved:** Which non-food baby safety products have a schema strong enough to override their ordinary product-form L1.
- **Recommendation impact:** Supports ROOT-02 Option A; does not decide all Anne & Bebek exceptions.

### ROOT-05 — Fixed installation versus movable product

- **Current official support:** TR-04 demonstrates a dedicated safety and conformity regime for gas-burning appliances/fittings. This is evidence that installation/interface fields can be safety-critical rather than cosmetic.
- **Taxonomy consequence:** Fixed connection, infrastructure dependency and professional-installation evidence are valid routing inputs. They do not automatically make every appliance a Hardware product.
- **Fail-closed gate:** Products involving gas, fixed mains connection or building integration require explicit connection/installation metadata and compliance evidence before listing.
- **Evidence required later:** Connection type, fixed/movable state, product-safety regime, conformity evidence and installation requirements.
- **Unresolved:** The exact retail allowlist and the boundary between the physical appliance, fitting and installation service.
- **Recommendation impact:** Supports the evidence gate in ROOT-05 Option A; the Hardware/Appliance owner remains a product decision.

### ROOT-07 — Generic versus fitment/device-specific electronics

- **Current official support:** TR-05 distinguishes charging service, public/commercial stations and non-commercial private units. It shows that a wallbox/charging product cannot be classified safely from the word “charger” alone.
- **Taxonomy consequence:** Vehicle fitment, fixed installation, commercial operation and generic multi-device use must be separate fields. Ordinary cable/powerbank semantics must not be conflated with EV charging infrastructure.
- **Fail-closed gate:** EV charging and hazardous/high-energy battery products require an exact product/evidence profile; no title-only routing.
- **Evidence required later:** Compatible vehicle/device, fixed or portable state, charging-service role, electrical characteristics, conformity and installer requirements.
- **Unresolved:** Wallbox owner, portable EVSE boundary, ordinary car adapter boundary and battery eligibility.
- **Recommendation impact:** Supports ROOT-07 Option A's evidence-based fitment rule; does not settle every Electronics/Automotive/Hardware case.

### ROOT-08 — PPE and certified protection ownership

- **Current official support:** TR-06 and TR-07 show that PPE is defined by protective intended use and essential safety/conformity duties, and that market surveillance remains active in 2026.
- **Taxonomy consequence:** Product shape alone is insufficient. Occupational PPE, medical PPE, sports protection and ordinary clothing/footwear/eyewear need different intended-use and evidence fields.
- **Fail-closed gate:** “Protective,” “safety” or similar merchant text is not certification evidence. Certified-function routing requires verifiable conformity and intended-use data.
- **Evidence required later:** Intended risk, PPE category/status, conformity/CE evidence, medical-device status if claimed and authorized documentation.
- **Unresolved:** Medical-versus-occupational PPE precedence and exact evidence vocabulary.
- **Recommendation impact:** Supports ROOT-08 Option A; does not auto-classify an individual helmet, mask, glove, shoe or eyewear SKU.

### ROOT-11 — Weapon-like and hazardous recreation posture

- **Current official support:** TR-08 confirms distinct legal regimes for firearms, hunting/sporting guns, blank-firing weapons and related implements. TR-09 confirms a dedicated pyrotechnic safety/conformity regime. TR-10 confirms alcohol seller authorization and electronic-commerce restrictions.
- **Taxonomy consequence:** Toy, sport, party, food or bag labels cannot override regulated status. These families need independent eligibility decisions, not merely category placement.
- **Fail-closed gate:** V1 exclusions remain. Any future inclusion needs a current, item-specific legal allowlist plus seller, age, fulfilment and moderation controls.
- **Evidence required later:** Exact legal family, authorization/licence, age rule, delivery/channel restriction, storage/transport rule and product conformity.
- **Unresolved:** Item-level allowlists for airsoft/paintball/hunting accessories, compressed/pressurized party products and any lawful-but-high-risk goods.
- **Recommendation impact:** Strongly supports ROOT-11 Option A and continued explicit exclusions; it does not declare an unnamed product lawful or unlawful.

### ROOT-12 — Live and biological product scope

- **Current official support:** TR-11 and TR-12 show current sales, storage, prescription and supervision rules for plant-protection products. TR-15 shows protected live species and derived materials may require permits or face commercial prohibitions.
- **Taxonomy consequence:** Live plant/seed, pesticide/plant-protection product, live animal and protected-species material are separate eligibility families even when they share a garden or pet use context.
- **Fail-closed gate:** Live animals and plant-protection products remain excluded unless an explicit owner/legal scope later enables them. Allowed plants/seeds still require provenance, fulfilment and any phytosanitary controls.
- **Evidence required later:** Live/non-live state, species, protected status, provenance, plant-protection active ingredient/status, prescription requirement and fulfilment conditions.
- **Unresolved:** Exact allowed live-plant/seed list, delivery/welfare capability and invasive/protected-species checks.
- **Recommendation impact:** Supports ROOT-12 Option A; does not authorize live inventory.

### ROOT-15 — Pet nutrition and veterinary-health rule

- **Current official support:** TR-13 confirms pet-feed labelling and disease/pest-claim constraints. TR-14 confirms veterinary medicines are a separate regulated family. These support distinct food, hygiene, supplement and medicine gates.
- **Taxonomy consequence:** Intended species is a stable browsing/routing input for ordinary pet food and supplies; regulated veterinary status remains a separate eligibility dimension.
- **Fail-closed gate:** Veterinary medicines, claimed supplements and biosidal/therapeutic products remain excluded or review-gated until exact item-level permission exists.
- **Evidence required later:** Intended species, feed category, label/claim data, veterinary medicinal-product status, authorization and seller eligibility.
- **Unresolved:** Pet supplement eligibility, biosidal/hygiene boundary and whether shared multi-species goods need a controlled L3 representation.
- **Recommendation impact:** Supports ROOT-15 Option A; does not finalize the species-first L2 ballot.

### ROOT-16 — Precious/high-value and protected-material provenance

- **Current official support:** TR-16 establishes a sector-specific authorization and operating framework for jewelry retail. TR-17 adds a current disclosure obligation for synthetic precious stones. TR-15 shows protected species materials require separate provenance/permit treatment.
- **Taxonomy consequence:** Product form can remain the owner while material, authenticity, synthetic/natural status, protected origin and seller authorization are policy/evidence fields.
- **Fail-closed gate:** Loose/investment precious products, unverifiable authenticity, ambiguous synthetic/natural claims and protected-material goods remain excluded or review-gated.
- **Evidence required later:** Seller authorization, product form, metal/stone identity, synthetic/natural disclosure, certificate/provenance and protected-species permit status.
- **Unresolved:** V1 treatment of investment products, loose stones, antiques and high-value or protected-material fulfilment.
- **Recommendation impact:** Supports ROOT-16 Option A; does not approve any high-value/protected-material assortment.

## Cross-root evidence conclusions

1. **Intended use is evidence, not a merchant-selected category shortcut.** This is especially important across ROOT-01, ROOT-08 and ROOT-15.
2. **Regulatory status and taxonomy ownership are separate axes.** A regulated product may retain its product-form owner, while a familiar-looking product may require a specialist evidence gate.
3. **Seller and channel eligibility can be stricter than product classification.** Alcohol, jewelry, medical devices, veterinary medicines and weapon-like goods illustrate this separation.
4. **Installation, fitment and service must not be collapsed.** ROOT-05, ROOT-07 and ROOT-10 need distinct physical-product and service/infrastructure facts.
5. **Protected or biological origin requires provenance.** ROOT-12 and ROOT-16 cannot be resolved safely from names or images alone.
6. **No official source reviewed supplies a complete EsnaftaVar V1 allowlist.** Every high-risk inclusion still needs an item-level legal/compliance artifact.
