# Compliance Contrarian Review

**State:** SCOPE CHALLENGE COMPLETE

## Where the foundation could overreach

- A 24-category legal clearance exercise is unnecessary if the first pilot excludes the affected
  regulated items and retains an item-level policy queue.
- A dedicated enterprise consent-management, GRC or case-management suite is not justified for a
  small owner-operated pilot; versioned records and auditable procedures are sufficient initially.
- Broad age-verification infrastructure is poor value if alcohol, tobacco/nicotine, weapons and
  other age-sensitive inventory is excluded.
- Behavioral ad profiling, cross-merchant rewards and automated moderation create more compliance
  surface than pilot value. Disabling them is a product simplification, not a missing control.
- Two-person approval for every ordinary action is ceremony. Reserve strong confirmation and
  independent review for irreversible/high-risk actions; log the rest.
- Retaining every event “for future analytics” is not evidence of necessity.

## Controls that cannot be simplified away

Accurate privacy notice, data minimization, rights/deletion handling, security/breach procedure,
regulated-product fail-closed rules, truthful sponsor/review/QR claims, merchant evidence and a
reachable complaint route remain real pilot dependencies.

## Result

The leanest defensible pilot is intentionally narrow. It does not require every future compliance
system, but every enabled capability must have a factual purpose, visible rule, accountable reviewer
and enforceable control.
