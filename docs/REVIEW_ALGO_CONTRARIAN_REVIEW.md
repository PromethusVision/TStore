# Contrarian Review

**State:** CHALLENGE FINDINGS — NOT OWNER APPROVAL

## Findings that narrow the proposal

1. Merchant reputation is **not necessary** for the first pilot; QR and product-review integrity deliver value
   without a badge program.
2. Structured questions should precede badges. Otherwise thresholds are designed without local response,
   sector and abuse distributions.
3. `Mahallenin Yıldızı` is premature: small neighborhood cohorts turn a relative label into an arbitrary winner.
4. Four dimensions may confuse or tire customers; three is the leading pilot candidate and one overall question
   is a defensible fallback.
5. Exponential recency decay is explainability-heavy without evidence it improves decisions; timestamps and a
   later rolling window are enough initially.
6. A public merchant star would duplicate product-star language and invite scope confusion. Narrow badges and
   distributions are safer if any reputation output launches.
7. Composite badges add little until primary badge comprehension and operations are proven.
8. Badges can create pressure, cold-start disadvantage and perceived platform guarantees; no-badge must remain
   neutral and organic discovery must remain available.
9. More mathematical sophistication is not automatically more fair. Bayesian priors can encode cohort bias,
   while lower-bound binary transforms discard 1–5 information.
10. Manual review everywhere is also unsafe and costly; deterministic versioned recomputation plus cases only
    for anomalies/disputes is the leaner design.

## What remains worth designing now

Evidence separation, identity, effective customer cap, event idempotency, question versioning, badge DAG and
appeal/audit boundaries are expensive to retrofit and can be specified without launching public reputation.
