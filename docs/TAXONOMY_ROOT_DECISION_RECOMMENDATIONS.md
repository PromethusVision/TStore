## Option A positions

### ROOT-01 — Medical intended-use rule

- **RECOMMENDED OPTION:** A
- **CONFIDENCE:** MEDIUM
- **POSITION:** Evidence-backed medical intended use/registration controls ownership; ordinary wellness/cosmetic/food products remain in their functional L1.
- **WHY:** It preserves one stable product owner while failing closed on regulated claims and eligibility.
- **TRADEOFF:** Adds evidence capture and moderation cost but prevents health claims from silently changing ownership.
- **WHAT REMAINS UNRESOLVED:** Exact medicine, supplement, medical-nutrition, device, optics and biosidal eligibility matrices.
- **LEGAL/POLICY REVIEW REQUIRED:** YES

### ROOT-02 — Baby/life-stage ownership rule

- **RECOMMENDED OPTION:** A
- **CONFIDENCE:** MEDIUM
- **POSITION:** Life-stage owner applies only when baby/pregnancy-specific function and schema are primary; ordinary form/size/marketing remains in the product L1.
- **WHY:** It recognizes genuine feeding, care and safety contracts without duplicating all child-sized products.
- **TRADEOFF:** Some baby-labeled products remain outside Anne & Bebek, requiring strong facets/search projection.
- **WHAT REMAINS UNRESOLVED:** Formula and safety-product legal gates; age-only exceptions.
- **LEGAL/POLICY REVIEW REQUIRED:** YES

### ROOT-03 — Technical sport product ownership

- **RECOMMENDED OPTION:** A
- **CONFIDENCE:** HIGH
- **POSITION:** Wearable form stays in Clothing/Shoes; Sports owns equipment and explicitly approved integrated technical systems.
- **WHY:** It preserves customer product identity while allowing safety/performance equipment to retain specialist schema.
- **TRADEOFF:** Customers may browse technical apparel through Clothing/Shoes rather than Sports; cross-domain discovery is required.
- **WHAT REMAINS UNRESOLVED:** Small allowlist of integrated technical sport systems.
- **LEGAL/POLICY REVIEW REQUIRED:** NO

### ROOT-04 — Generic versus domain-specific carrying product

- **RECOMMENDED OPTION:** A
- **CONFIDENCE:** HIGH
- **POSITION:** Standalone carrying product stays in Bags; inseparable or schema-changing integrated technical module follows the specialist product.
- **WHY:** Compatibility is normally a facet, while true integration can change product identity.
- **TRADEOFF:** Standalone specialist cases remain in Bags, so compatibility filtering must be strong.
- **WHAT REMAINS UNRESOLVED:** Evidence threshold for inseparable/integrated carrying modules.
- **LEGAL/POLICY REVIEW REQUIRED:** NO

### ROOT-05 — Fixed installation versus movable product

- **RECOMMENDED OPTION:** A
- **CONFIDENCE:** HIGH
- **POSITION:** Fixed building connection/installation routes to Hardware; movable/decorative or plant-primary product remains in its functional L1.
- **WHY:** Installation and infrastructure create a stable schema boundary without turning rooms into categories.
- **TRADEOFF:** Installation metadata adds onboarding questions for borderline fixtures.
- **WHAT REMAINS UNRESOLVED:** Retail allowlist for gas/HVAC/mains and custom-install projects.
- **LEGAL/POLICY REVIEW REQUIRED:** YES

### ROOT-06 — Manual versus powered household product

- **RECOMMENDED OPTION:** A
- **CONFIDENCE:** HIGH
- **POSITION:** Powered finished appliance follows Home Appliances; manual utensil/consumable remains Kitchen/Home/Cosmetics.
- **WHY:** Device lifecycle, safety and compatibility differ from manual products; smart connectivity does not change appliance ownership.
- **TRADEOFF:** Power source becomes a decisive prompt even when customer use context is identical.
- **WHAT REMAINS UNRESOLVED:** Device-specific powered accessory and electrical evidence profiles.
- **LEGAL/POLICY REVIEW REQUIRED:** NO

### ROOT-07 — Generic versus fitment/device-specific electronics

- **RECOMMENDED OPTION:** A
- **CONFIDENCE:** HIGH
- **POSITION:** Verified vehicle/device/tool/building fitment and primary function control; otherwise generic product remains in owner-final Electronics/Computer owner.
- **WHY:** It extends the canonical generic/specific accessory rule without reopening final Electronics/Computer structures.
- **TRADEOFF:** Merchants must provide fitment/integration evidence; generic fallbacks may feel conservative.
- **WHAT REMAINS UNRESOLVED:** EV wallbox, hazardous battery and generic car-adapter thresholds.
- **LEGAL/POLICY REVIEW REQUIRED:** YES

### ROOT-08 — PPE and certified protection ownership

- **RECOMMENDED OPTION:** A
- **CONFIDENCE:** MEDIUM
- **POSITION:** Certified occupational protection follows Hardware/OHS; medical PPE follows Health; sport protection follows Sports; ordinary form remains Clothing/Shoes/Optics.
- **WHY:** Intended use and certification schema distinguish protection systems from ordinary products.
- **TRADEOFF:** Certified intent can override familiar product form and may require review queues.
- **WHAT REMAINS UNRESOLVED:** Certification vocabulary and medical-versus-OHS PPE eligibility.
- **LEGAL/POLICY REVIEW REQUIRED:** YES

### ROOT-09 — Gift purpose and personalization no-duplication rule

- **RECOMMENDED OPTION:** A
- **CONFIDENCE:** HIGH
- **POSITION:** Underlying product retains ownership; personalization/gift/occasion is facet or merchant capability; only intrinsic keepsake is candidate Gift product.
- **WHY:** It eliminates global duplication while retaining genuine keepsake and party products.
- **TRADEOFF:** Hediyelik loses broad product ownership and relies more on collections.
- **WHAT REMAINS UNRESOLVED:** Intrinsic-keepsake definition and multi-line bundle representation.
- **LEGAL/POLICY REVIEW REQUIRED:** NO

### ROOT-10 — Product versus service boundary

- **RECOMMENDED OPTION:** A
- **CONFIDENCE:** HIGH
- **POSITION:** Classify a separately sellable principal physical product; keep labor/time/subscription outside Product Taxonomy.
- **WHY:** It preserves the canonical Product/Merchant/Service separation without losing real physical products.
- **TRADEOFF:** Merchant services cannot be represented as ordinary product leaves yet.
- **WHAT REMAINS UNRESOLVED:** Future service taxonomy, service pricing and optional-service offer model.
- **LEGAL/POLICY REVIEW REQUIRED:** NO

### ROOT-11 — Weapon-like and hazardous recreation posture

- **RECOMMENDED OPTION:** A
- **CONFIDENCE:** MEDIUM
- **POSITION:** Preserve explicit exclusions; use exact legal allowlist for any non-excluded weapon-like family.
- **WHY:** Familiar toy/sport/party labels cannot be treated as legal eligibility evidence.
- **TRADEOFF:** Fail-closed posture sacrifices some potentially lawful assortment until legal/product controls exist.
- **WHAT REMAINS UNRESOLVED:** Authoritative item-by-item legal allowlists; recommendation remains fail closed.
- **LEGAL/POLICY REVIEW REQUIRED:** YES

### ROOT-12 — Live and biological product scope

- **RECOMMENDED OPTION:** A
- **CONFIDENCE:** MEDIUM
- **POSITION:** Live animals and prohibited plant-protection products remain excluded; allowed live plants/seeds use exact traceability/fulfilment gates.
- **WHY:** Product families differ materially in legal status, welfare, seasonality and fulfilment risk.
- **TRADEOFF:** Live local-retail assortment stays limited until fulfilment/traceability gates are ready.
- **WHAT REMAINS UNRESOLVED:** Species/product traceability, fulfilment and pesticide/live-animal exclusions.
- **LEGAL/POLICY REVIEW REQUIRED:** YES

### ROOT-13 — Facet or controlled category exception

- **RECOMMENDED OPTION:** A
- **CONFIDENCE:** HIGH
- **POSITION:** Permit only when a stable, non-overlapping product schema and deterministic registry exist; otherwise facet/projection.
- **WHY:** It prevents marketing dimensions from duplicating product leaves while allowing rare schema-bearing exceptions.
- **TRADEOFF:** A strict exception test may remove familiar merchandising labels from canonical L2.
- **WHAT REMAINS UNRESOLVED:** Which schema-bearing age/culture collections deserve a controlled exception.
- **LEGAL/POLICY REVIEW REQUIRED:** NO

### ROOT-14 — Published content, supply and kit principal-product rule

- **RECOMMENDED OPTION:** A
- **CONFIDENCE:** HIGH
- **POSITION:** Published readable/instructional content → Books; blank/general supplies → Stationery; complete guided play/experiment object → Toys.
- **WHY:** Principal product and user activity are more reliable than component count or ISBN alone.
- **TRADEOFF:** Principal-product metadata is needed for mixed activity products.
- **WHAT REMAINS UNRESOLVED:** Mixed physical bundles and non-ISBN activity content edge cases.
- **LEGAL/POLICY REVIEW REQUIRED:** NO

### ROOT-15 — Pet nutrition and veterinary-health rule

- **RECOMMENDED OPTION:** A
- **CONFIDENCE:** MEDIUM
- **POSITION:** Animal-intended ordinary supplies/food stay Pet; medical/supplement/biosidal families require controlled veterinary scope and may remain excluded.
- **WHY:** Intended species is primary, while veterinary status remains a separate legal gate.
- **TRADEOFF:** Species-first browsing may coexist awkwardly with shared-product ownership until L3 is designed.
- **WHAT REMAINS UNRESOLVED:** Veterinary medicine/supplement eligibility and species-first structural ballot.
- **LEGAL/POLICY REVIEW REQUIRED:** YES

### ROOT-16 — Precious/high-value and protected-material provenance

- **RECOMMENDED OPTION:** A
- **CONFIDENCE:** MEDIUM
- **POSITION:** Keep product-form owner; apply provenance, authenticity, seller and secure-commerce gates.
- **WHY:** Material/value are attributes and policy risks, not stable product identity.
- **TRADEOFF:** Taxonomy remains simple, but high-value/provenance review stays operationally expensive.
- **WHAT REMAINS UNRESOLVED:** Investment products, loose stones and protected-material evidence.
- **LEGAL/POLICY REVIEW REQUIRED:** YES

### ROOT-17 — Same-L1 primary-intent rule for future L3/L4

- **RECOMMENDED OPTION:** A
- **CONFIDENCE:** HIGH
- **POSITION:** Use domain-specific deterministic primary-intent tests; keep synonyms/facets separate and forbid dual primary leaves.
- **WHY:** It preserves current L2 architecture while making later L3/L4 assignment testable.
- **TRADEOFF:** One global rule still needs domain-specific precedence details at L3/L4.
- **WHAT REMAINS UNRESOLVED:** Domain-specific L3/L4 precedence ladders.
- **LEGAL/POLICY REVIEW REQUIRED:** NO
